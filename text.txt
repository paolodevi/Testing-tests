EQUIVALENT PITCH DAMPING IDENTIFICATION FOR A REDUCED-ORDER LONGITUDINAL LANDING MODEL
======================================================================================

1. OBJECTIVE
------------

The objective is to replace a high-fidelity longitudinal landing simulation with a
simplified linear dynamic model that can be used for fast simulations, parameter
studies, optimization, Monte Carlo analyses or preliminary design activities.

The original model includes several strongly coupled subsystems:

- Aircraft rigid-body longitudinal dynamics
- Aerodynamic forces and moments
- Landing gear dynamics (oleo, tires, structural compliance)
- Flight Control System (FCS)
- Gravity
- Possible structural flexibility

Because all of these systems interact during touchdown, the resulting pitching motion
contains damping contributions from several physical mechanisms. The purpose is not
to identify the aerodynamic pitch damping derivative alone, but rather to identify
an equivalent damping that reproduces the overall dynamic behavior of the complete
system.

The final product should therefore be a reduced-order model capable of reproducing
the pitch response of the original simulation over the range of landing conditions
of interest.


2. WHY A SINGLE DAMPING COEFFICIENT DOES NOT EXIST
-------------------------------------------------

In classical flight dynamics, pitch damping is represented by the aerodynamic
derivative Cm_q, which produces a pitching moment proportional to the pitch rate.

    M_q = (dM/dq) q

or, in non-dimensional form,

    Cm = Cm0 + Cm_alpha * alpha + Cm_q * q + ...

This description is valid only for small perturbations around a fixed trim condition.

A landing impact is fundamentally different.

During touchdown:

- Airspeed changes continuously.
- Angle of attack changes.
- Landing gear compresses and rebounds.
- Tire forces vary rapidly.
- The FCS may command elevator inputs.
- Load factor varies significantly.
- Pitch rate can become relatively large.

Consequently, the total pitching moment is

    M_total =
        M_aero
      + M_gear
      + M_gravity
      + M_control
      + ...

Each contribution depends on different variables.

For example,

    M_aero = f(alpha, q, V, delta_e, ...)

while

    M_gear = f(stroke, stroke_rate, tire_force, ...)

Therefore there is generally no constant coefficient satisfying

    M_total = M0 + C q

for every landing condition.

The damping is nonlinear and varies throughout the touchdown event.


3. WHAT SHOULD THE REDUCED MODEL REPRESENT?
-------------------------------------------

The objective is not to reproduce each physical subsystem individually.

Instead, the reduced model should reproduce the overall aircraft motion.

A suitable reduced-order model is

    Iyy * theta_ddot
    + C_eq * theta_dot
    + K_eq * theta
    = M_external

where

Iyy     = aircraft pitch inertia
C_eq    = equivalent pitch damping
K_eq    = equivalent pitch stiffness

The equivalent damping should represent the combined effect of

- aerodynamic damping
- landing gear damping
- tire damping
- controller action
- any additional dissipative mechanism

without attempting to separate them.

This is similar to equivalent damping used in structural dynamics, where several
physical damping mechanisms are represented by one viscous damper.


4. IDENTIFICATION OF EQUIVALENT PARAMETERS
------------------------------------------

For every landing simulation, extract

- pitch angle          theta(t)
- pitch rate           q(t)
- pitch acceleration   q_dot(t)

and, if available,

- total pitching moment

The rigid-body equation is

    Iyy q_dot = M_total

Assuming local linear behavior,

    Iyy q_dot
    =
    a theta
    +
    b q
    +
    c

where

a = equivalent stiffness

b = equivalent damping

c = equilibrium offset

The coefficients can be identified using least-squares regression over the portion
of the simulation corresponding to the touchdown dynamics.

This provides one equivalent damping coefficient for one landing condition.


5. WHY MULTIPLE SIMULATIONS ARE NECESSARY
-----------------------------------------

Since the damping changes with operating conditions, repeating the identification
for many landing cases will produce different values of C_eq.

Rather than searching for one universal damping coefficient, the objective becomes
the identification of a damping map.

Each simulation produces

    Landing conditions
        ->
    Equivalent damping

The equivalent damping therefore becomes a function

    C_eq = f(landing conditions)


6. CHOICE OF INDEPENDENT VARIABLES
---------------------------------

The choice of independent variables is critical.

The variables should strongly influence the pitch dynamics while remaining relatively
few in number.

A reasonable initial set is

- aircraft mass
- sink rate
- touchdown load factor (Nz)

These parameters directly influence the landing energy and therefore the dynamic
response.

Additional variables that may become important include

- touchdown airspeed
- pitch attitude
- pitch rate at touchdown
- angle of attack
- center of gravity position
- flap configuration
- landing gear characteristics

Sensitivity analyses can determine whether each variable significantly affects the
identified damping.

If a variable has negligible influence, it can be removed from the parameterization.


7. BUILDING THE DAMPING MAP
---------------------------

For every simulation,

    Inputs

        Mass
        Sink rate
        Nz
        ...

    Output

        C_eq

After generating enough data, fit a response surface

    C_eq = f(mass, sink rate, Nz)

Possible fitting techniques include

- linear regression
- polynomial regression
- response surface methodology
- radial basis functions
- Gaussian process regression
- multidimensional interpolation

The choice depends on the smoothness and complexity of the data.

The resulting map allows the reduced model to automatically select an equivalent
damping corresponding to the simulated landing condition.


8. ALTERNATIVE IDENTIFICATION USING ENERGY
------------------------------------------

Instead of identifying damping from the equations of motion, an equivalent damping
may be defined by requiring the reduced model to dissipate the same amount of energy
as the original model.

The energy dissipated during one oscillation is

    DeltaE = integral(M dtheta)

For a viscous damper

    DeltaE
    =
    C_eq
    integral(q² dt)

Therefore

    C_eq
    =
    DeltaE
    /
    integral(q² dt)

This method is attractive because

- it naturally accounts for nonlinear behavior
- it avoids numerical differentiation
- it produces one equivalent damping for the entire landing event

rather than an instantaneous coefficient.

This approach is commonly used in nonlinear structural dynamics.


9. INSTANTANEOUS VS EVENT-EQUIVALENT DAMPING
--------------------------------------------

Two different concepts should be distinguished.

Instantaneous damping

- identified over small time windows
- varies during touchdown
- useful for understanding the physics
- generally depends on the current operating point

Event-equivalent damping

- one value representing the entire landing
- chosen to reproduce the overall response
- more appropriate for simplified engineering models

Since the objective is model reduction, the event-equivalent damping is generally
the preferred quantity.


10. VALIDATION OF THE REDUCED MODEL
-----------------------------------

Identifying equivalent damping is only the first step.

The reduced model must then be validated against the original simulation.

Validation quantities include

- pitch angle history
- pitch rate history
- maximum pitch angle
- maximum pitch rate
- oscillation frequency
- settling time
- peak landing loads
- energy dissipation

The reduced model should reproduce these quantities with acceptable accuracy over
the intended range of landing conditions.

If significant discrepancies remain, additional state variables or nonlinear terms
may be required.


11. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
------------------------

Step 1

Generate a matrix of landing simulations covering the expected operational envelope.

Step 2

For every simulation, extract

- theta(t)
- q(t)
- q_dot(t)
- total pitching moment (if available)

Step 3

Identify

- equivalent stiffness
- equivalent damping

using least-squares regression or energy equivalence.

Step 4

Associate every identified coefficient with the corresponding landing conditions.

Step 5

Construct response surfaces

    C_eq = f(mass, sink rate, Nz)

and, if necessary,

    K_eq = g(mass, sink rate, Nz)

Step 6

Implement the reduced linear model using the identified parameters.

Step 7

Compare the reduced model with the original simulation over an independent set of
landing cases.

Step 8

Evaluate the prediction errors and refine the parameterization if necessary.


12. FINAL REMARKS
-----------------

The key point is that the objective is not to identify a classical aerodynamic pitch
damping derivative (Cm_q). Instead, the goal is to identify an equivalent damping
coefficient that encapsulates the combined dynamic effects of aerodynamics, landing
gear, flight controls, and other dissipative mechanisms. Such a coefficient will
not be universal but will depend on the landing conditions. Consequently, the most
robust reduced-order model is expected to use a parameterized damping map rather
than a single constant value. This approach preserves the computational efficiency
of a linear model while retaining the essential dynamic behavior of the original
high-fidelity landing simulation.
